Google Ads Statistics 2026: 50+ Numbers, Including Our Own Audit Data

For founders and marketing heads who want to know if their Google Ads numbers are good, bad or wasted. Every number has a source and a date.

By Suraj Donthi, Founder, ElementalsAI LabsUpdated 14 Oct 202613 min read
Short answer

In 2026, a Google search ad costs $5.42 per click on average, gets a 6.64% click-through rate, converts 8.18% of clicks and costs $66.69 per lead (LocaliQ, April 2025 – March 2026 data). Waste is the bigger story: studies find 22–39% of search-term spend buys nothing. Our own audit found 48%.

Google told one store it had 816,270 conversions in nine weeks. The store shipped 303 orders. Same account. Same weeks.

That gap is why this page exists. Benchmarks tell you what average looks like. They do not tell you if your own numbers are real. Below: 70+ numbers, each with its source and date. Our own audit data comes last, in its own section.

How big is Google Ads in 2026?

Google Search is still the largest ad channel online: $63.3 billion of search ad revenue in one quarter.

  • $63.3 billion: Google Search & other revenue, Q2 2026, up 17% from $54.2 billion. Alphabet Q2 2026 release, 22 Jul 2026.
  • $81.6 billion: all Google advertising revenue, Q2 2026, up from $71.3 billion a year before. Same source.
  • $11.1 billion: YouTube ad revenue, Q2 2026, up 13%. Same source.
  • $7.30 billion: Google Network revenue (ads on partner sites and apps), Q2 2026, down from $7.35 billion. Same source.
  • 89.94%: Google's share of worldwide search, September 2026. Bing has 5.29%. StatCounter.
  • 1 billion+: monthly users of Google's AI Mode, July 2026. Alphabet Q2 2026 earnings call, 22 Jul 2026.

Sundar Pichai, Alphabet's CEO, put it plainly: "Our popular AI features are driving Search query growth." (Alphabet, Jul 2026). More searches means more auctions. It does not mean more profit for you.

What is the average CPC on Google Ads in 2026?

The average cost per click (CPC) on Google search ads is $5.42 in 2026, across 23 industries. Prices barely moved this year. Clicks grew instead.

  • $5.42: average CPC, all industries. LocaliQ 2026 benchmarks (a vendor: its own customers' Google and Microsoft campaigns), data Apr 2025 – Mar 2026.
  • $9.87: highest average CPC, Attorneys & Legal Services. Same source.
  • $1.63: lowest average CPC, Arts & Entertainment. Same source.
  • +27.27%: biggest CPC rise year on year, Real Estate. Search Engine Journal, 16 Jun 2026.
  • +1%: Google search CPC change, Q2 2026 vs Q2 2025. Clicks grew 13% and spend 14%. Tinuiti (an agency; managed accounts), via Karooya, 11 Aug 2026.
  • −1%: Google Shopping CPC change, Q2 2026, while Shopping spend grew 18%. Same source.
  • +19%: Microsoft Advertising CPC change, Q2 2026. Same source.
  • 2×: Google can spend up to twice your average daily budget on one day. The monthly cap is 30.4× the daily budget. Google Ads Help.

Most teams stare at CPC. Winners stare at cost per sale. A cheap click that never buys is the most expensive click you have. If you pay someone to run the account, add their fee to this number: see Google Ads management cost in 2026.

What is a good CTR for Google Ads in 2026?

A good click-through rate (CTR: the share of people who see your ad and click it) beats 6.64%, the 2026 average. AI Overviews now pull that number down on question-type searches.

  • 6.64%: average search CTR, all industries. LocaliQ, data Apr 2025 – Mar 2026.
  • 12.75%: highest average CTR, Arts & Entertainment. Same source.
  • 5.56%: lowest average CTR, Automotive Repair, Service & Parts. Same source.
  • +31.71%: biggest CTR rise year on year, Education & Instruction. Search Engine Journal, Jun 2026.
  • −68%: paid CTR on informational searches that show an AI Overview, 19.70% (Jun 2024) to 6.34% (Sep 2025). An agency study by Seer Interactive: 3,119 search terms, 42 clients, 4 Nov 2025.
  • −32%: paid CTR on similar searches with no AI Overview, 19.1% to 13.04%, same period. Same source.
  • 91%: more paid clicks for brands cited inside the AI Overview than for brands not cited, Q3 2025. Same source. Seer says this shows a link, not proof of cause.

What is a good conversion rate and cost per lead on Google Ads?

The average Google Ads conversion rate is 8.18% and the average cost per lead is $66.69 in 2026. Cost per lead fell for the first time in five years.

  • 8.18%: average Google Ads conversion rate, all industries. LocaliQ, data Apr 2025 – Mar 2026.
  • 16.22%: highest average conversion rate, Animals & Pets. Same source.
  • 2.64%: lowest average conversion rate, Finance & Insurance. Same source.
  • $66.69: average cost per lead, all industries. Same source.
  • $131.63: highest average cost per lead, Attorneys & Legal Services. Same source.
  • $26.84: lowest average cost per lead, Arts & Entertainment. Same source.
  • 5 years: the first overall fall in cost per lead in that long. Same source.
  • −39.35%: biggest cost-per-lead drop year on year, Travel. Search Engine Journal, Jun 2026.
  • 7.41% vs 1.45%: conversion rate of landing pages scored 9–10 vs pages scored 5 or below (2,633 and 2,098 pages). A vendor study by Doctor Ads, 16 Aug 2026.

These rates assume a "conversion" is a sale or a lead. Check that first. In our audit, Google counted page views as purchases, which is mistake #1 in 12 common Google Ads mistakes. A fake conversion rate makes every other number on this page useless.

How much Google Ads spend is wasted?

In most studies, 22% to 39% of search-term spend buys zero conversions. In our audit, 48% of the whole budget sold nothing. The waste is concentrated, so you can find it.

  • 29.0%: share of paid search spend on search terms with zero conversions. A vendor study by Doctor Ads, exports mostly Sep 2024 – Jan 2025.
  • 48.8%: share of that waste in the worst tenth of search terms. Same source.
  • 39.4%: median advertiser's share of tracked search-term cost on zero-conversion terms, 84 advertisers, 2023–2025. Doctor Ads, updated 28 Sep 2026.
  • 22.3%: the same measure weighted by spend. Bigger budgets waste a smaller share. Same source.
  • 6% to nearly 40%: waste in the best-run vs worst-run account within one industry. Same source.
  • r = −0.10: the link between negative-keyword list size and waste, close to none. Same source.
  • 36.1%: average waste across 43 enterprise B2B SaaS accounts ($31.2M yearly spend), range 28.1–45.1%, Aug 2024 – Jul 2025. A vendor report by GrowthSpree.
  • 89.7%: waste rate on bids for competitors' brand names. Same source.
  • 67.8%: waste rate on overnight spend (9 pm – 6 am). Same source.
  • 6.4%: extra cost per conversion on broad match vs exact match, median account. Doctor Ads, Aug 2026.
  • 5.58%: share of 4.006 billion paid ad clicks flagged as fraud in H1 2026, up from 4.32% in 2025. Spider AF (a fraud-detection vendor), 2 Sep 2026.
  • $25.3 billion: its estimate of ad money lost to fraud worldwide in H1 2026. Same source.

Most teams answer waste with a longer negative-keyword list. The data says that barely helps. Winners cut by product, by asset and by campaign, the three levels where waste hides. Every check is in our Google Ads audit checklist.

How does Performance Max perform?

Google says Performance Max (PMax: one campaign that buys Search, Shopping, YouTube, Display and more) adds conversions. Independent account data says it misses its targets more often than Search.

  • 27%: more conversions or value at a similar cost per action for advertisers who adopt PMax. Google's own data, Oct – Nov 2023, Google Ads Help.
  • 15%: more conversions or value on Search at a similar return for advertisers using AI Max or PMax. Philipp Schindler, Google, on the Q2 2026 earnings call, 22 Jul 2026.
  • 71.3% vs 26.3%: share of campaigns more than 10% over their cost-per-acquisition target: 178 PMax vs 1,563 Search campaigns. A vendor study by Doctor Ads, Aug 2026. It shows a pattern, not proof of cause.
  • 60%: PMax share of spend across Shopping and PMax for advertisers running both, Q2 2026, down from 67% in Q1. Tinuiti via Karooya.
  • 71%: share of fraud losses on made-for-advertising sites that ran through AI-run channels such as PMax, H1 2026, up from about 62% in 2025. Spider AF.
  • 82%: advertisers that run PMax next to other campaign types. A vendor study by Optmyzr, 9,199 accounts, 7 Oct 2024.
  • 97%: advertisers in that study not using brand list exclusions in PMax. Same source.

The lesson: PMax spends where you let it. Read the channel split every month. We compare both campaign types in Performance Max vs Search campaigns.

How many advertisers use AI and automation in Google Ads?

Nearly all of them. More than 80% used automated bidding already in 2021, and 500,000 advertisers have turned on AI Max.

  • 80%+: Google advertisers using automated bidding. Google internal data, Mar – Apr 2021, Google Ads Help. We found no newer Google figure.
  • 500,000: advertisers that adopted AI Max for Search, now out of beta. Philipp Schindler, Q2 2026 earnings call, 22 Jul 2026.
  • 14%: typical extra conversions or value at a similar cost when AI Max is on. Google Ads blog, 6 May 2025.
  • 27%: the typical lift for campaigns still running mostly exact and phrase keywords. Same source.
  • 15%: more clicks and conversions on average when Ad Strength goes from Poor to Excellent. Google internal data, Aug 2025, Google Ads Help.
  • 6.6%: more conversions at a similar cost when advertisers with one responsive search ad add a second. Same source.
  • 3.7%: further lift from a third responsive search ad. Same source.

Most teams switch the AI on and walk away. Winners feed it clean signals and check it every week. The AI bids for whatever you tell it is a conversion. Tell it a page view is a sale, and it buys page views. We tested the tools that help in best AI tools for Google Ads.

What did one real audit find?

One store lost 48% of its Google Ads budget to ads that sold nothing. Here is the original data. We audited one Indian D2C (direct-to-consumer) store: every campaign since April 2023, 5,788 store orders, data to 9 Oct 2026. The brand stays private. "Proven" means a direct count from the account or the store.

  • 48%: share of a ₹71,800 monthly budget that sold nothing, ₹34,400 a month. Proven.
  • ₹16,900 + ₹17,500: the split: ads on products that never sell, plus YouTube video ads that show no product. Proven as waste; the YouTube saving needs a switch-off test.
  • +96% vs +31%: ad spend (₹37,247 → ₹73,133 a month) vs online revenue (₹246,748 → ₹324,135), Oct – Jul vs the year before. Proven.
  • 816,270 vs 303: Google "conversions" vs real store orders, 7 Aug – 9 Oct 2026. Page views were counted as purchases. Proven.
  • 32,485 vs 5: one PMax campaign's "conversions" in one week vs its real sales. Proven.
  • 64–89%: share of the value Google bid for that came from page views, every month since July 2025. Proven.
  • 234 of 450: store orders in 3 months that matched a Google purchase. Google saw about half. Proven.
  • 91%: store orders with no recorded source. Proven.
  • 792 of 1,027: products that took ₹51,100 in 3 months (41% of product-ad money) and sold nothing through ads. Proven.
  • 94%: share of ad sales from the top 10% of products. Proven.
  • ₹10.60 vs ₹0.01: sales per ₹1 of product ads on Search vs YouTube, 3 months. YouTube still got 56% of that money. Proven.
  • 47% → 13%: Search share of one PMax campaign's money after its target return was raised on 7–8 Aug 2026. Proven.
  • ₹648 → ₹988: cost per Google sale after targets went from 4 to 8–10; Google sales fell 40%. The fall is proven; a goal change in the same week shares the blame.
  • 1 in 5: eligible searches where the ads showed. About 70–80% were lost to ad rank; budget lost less than 15% in 22 of 24 months. Proven.
  • 64 of 79: images that made no sale, 4 running campaigns, 3 months. Proven.
  • 13 of 16: videos that made no sale. Proven.
  • 3,533 vs 1,080,000: views for Google's 101 auto-made images vs the store's own images, 8 months. Proven.
  • ₹500 vs ₹2,000–2,600: cost per sale at night (12 am – 8 am) at about ₹9,900 vs ₹19,000 of monthly night spend. Proven.
  • 74 of 761: products that could not show: out of stock, wrong price, bad image or broken page. Proven.
  • ₹34,000–70,000: extra store revenue a month we estimate the fixes can bring, on a ₹42,400–59,900 budget. Likely ₹34,000; possible ₹70,000.

Questions people ask

What is the average CPC on Google Ads in 2026?

The average cost per click on search ads is $5.42 across 23 industries (LocaliQ, data April 2025 to March 2026). Legal services pay the most at $9.87. Arts and entertainment pay the least at $1.63. Agency data from Tinuiti shows Google search CPCs rose only 1% year on year in Q2 2026.

What is a good conversion rate for Google Ads?

The 2026 average is 8.18%. A good rate beats the average for your industry, which runs from 2.64% in finance and insurance to 16.22% in animals and pets. First check that a conversion is a real sale or lead. In our audit, Google counted 816,270 conversions while the store had 303 orders.

What is a good CTR for Google Ads in 2026?

The 2026 average click-through rate for search ads is 6.64%. Industry averages run from 5.56% in automotive repair to 12.75% in arts and entertainment. Expect lower rates on questions that show an AI Overview: one agency study found paid CTR on those searches fell 68% between June 2024 and September 2025.

How much of a Google Ads budget is wasted?

Most studies find that 22% to 39% of search-term spend buys zero conversions. A Doctor Ads study of 84 advertisers found a median of 39.4%, or 22.3% weighted by spend. A GrowthSpree study of B2B SaaS accounts found 36.1%. Our own audit of one Indian D2C store found 48% of the whole budget sold nothing.

Is Performance Max better than Search campaigns?

It depends on your data. Google says advertisers who adopt Performance Max get 27% more conversions or value at a similar cost. A Doctor Ads study found 71.3% of Performance Max campaigns ran more than 10% over their cost-per-acquisition target, against 26.3% of Search campaigns. Test it against your own sales.

What is the average cost per lead on Google Ads?

The 2026 average cost per lead for search ads is $66.69 (LocaliQ). It runs from $26.84 in arts and entertainment to $131.63 in legal services. LocaliQ reports that cost per lead fell overall for the first time in five years.
