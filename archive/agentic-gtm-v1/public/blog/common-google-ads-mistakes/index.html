12 Common Google Ads Mistakes That Waste Your Budget (From a Real Audit)

For founders and marketing heads who run Google Ads: the 12 mistakes that burned money in one real account, with the numbers, the fix and the menu path.

By Suraj Donthi, Founder, ElementalsAI LabsUpdated 14 Oct 202612 min read
Short answer

The Google Ads mistakes that waste the most budget are counting page views as purchases, jumping your ROAS target, letting Performance Max pour money into YouTube, and advertising products that never sell. In one real audit of an Indian D2C store, two of these alone wasted 48% of a ₹71,800 monthly budget: ₹34,400 a month.

₹34,400 a month. That is what one Indian D2C (direct-to-consumer) store paid Google Ads for nothing: 48% of its ₹71,800 monthly budget.

Over a year, ad spend rose 96%. Online revenue rose 31%. Google still reported wins: 816,270 "conversions" from 7 Aug to 9 Oct 2026. The store had 303 orders.

We logged 145 findings in that audit (see the real numbers). These 12 cost the most or have the clearest proof, across three levels: campaign, asset and product.

Why is my Google Ads budget wasted?

Your budget is wasted when the account chases the wrong result and nobody checks what it buys. Here, ₹16,900 a month went to products that never sold through an ad, and ₹17,500 to YouTube ads with no product. GrowthSpree, a vendor, put average waste at 36.1% across 43 B2B SaaS accounts (GrowthSpree). More in Google Ads statistics 2026.

Two kinds of mistake live here. Leaks waste money: cut them to save. Bad changes cost sales: undo them to grow. The leaks were as big in the best months, so cutting waste alone won't bring sales back.

1. Counting page views as purchases

What happened. The page_view action was set to "Primary", so it steered the bids. From 7 Aug to 9 Oct 2026, Google counted 816,270 "conversions". The store had 303 orders.

Why it burns money. Smart Bidding (Google's automatic bidding) buys more of whatever you count. Page views are cheap. So Google bought page views and called it success.

The fix. Goals › Conversions › Summary › Edit goal. Set page_view, user_engagement and form_start to "Secondary (observe only)" (Google Ads Help). Keep "Purchases" as each campaign's only goal. Do it 2–3 weeks after the target fix.

How sure. The miscount is proven. That it lowered sales is not proven: the budget doubled at the same time.

How common. GrowthSpree, a vendor, found a significant tracking issue in 70–80% of the 300+ B2B SaaS accounts it audited (GrowthSpree, 2026).

2. Jumping your ROAS target overnight

What happened. Target ROAS (return on ad spend) tells Google how many rupees of sales to find for each ₹1. On 7–8 Aug 2026, 6 campaigns jumped to 7.3–11.5. Search spend fell 30%, Google sales 40%. The cost of each Google sale went from ₹648 to ₹988.

Why it burns money. A high target makes Google chase cheap results it can count: YouTube page views, not Search buyers. Google warns: "Overly-aggressive targets can affect your volume" (Google Ads Help).

The fix. Campaign › Settings › Bidding. Set target ROAS to 400–500%; the best months ran at 4.3. Never raise it more than 20% at once.

How sure. The jump and the fall are proven. The cause is shared with a goal change in the same week. Change one thing, then wait 2–3 weeks.

How common. In a Doctor Ads vendor study, 71.3% of 178 Performance Max campaigns missed their CPA target by over 10% (Doctor Ads).

3. Letting Performance Max pour money into YouTube

What happened. In 3 months, ₹53,100 went to YouTube ads with no product: 207,000 clicks for ₹312 of sales. That is ₹17,500 a month. Product ads earned ₹10.60 per ₹1 on Search and ₹0.01 on YouTube. Yet YouTube got 56% of product-ad money.

Why it burns money. YouTube brings 60,000–87,000 page views a week. With page views as a goal, it looks like a winner.

The fix. Fix mistakes 1 and 2 first. Then pull the no-product videos from one campaign for 3–4 weeks (Campaigns › Asset groups › Edit › Videos) and compare orders. Each month, check Search gets 30% or more in Campaigns › Insights and reports › Channel performance. Keep Display; here more Display spend went with more store revenue. More in Performance Max vs Search.

How sure. The spend and sales are proven. Whether viewers buy later through Search is not known, so the ₹17,500 saving is "up to", after the test.

How common. The agency bmg360 found video took 4% of Performance Max spend and 1% of conversions across 61 accounts (undated) (bmg360). Here, YouTube took 48% of all spend.

4. Replacing campaigns that still sell

What happened. Three campaigns made 131 sales in May 2026. In mid-June they were replaced. It happened twice more. The newest spent ₹21,600 for 5 sales. The best month ever, 150 Google sales in April 2026, came from campaigns that ran steadily for 9 months.

Why it burns money. A new campaign learns from zero. In its first weeks, only 0–27% of its money goes to Search.

The fix. Freeze the lineup until targets settle. Check Campaigns › Change history before any pause. Replacing one? Run both for 2 weeks. Judge on 8–12 weeks.

How sure. The swaps and sales are proven. "Wait 4–6 weeks before you judge" is likely.

How common. Google says bidding can take up to around 50 conversion events or 3 conversion cycles to calibrate (Google Ads Help). Every restart pays that bill.

5. Paying full price for night clicks

What happened. In April and May, about ₹9,900 a month of night ads (12 am – 8 am) brought sales at about ₹500 each. In June, July and September, about ₹19,000 a month brought sales at about ₹2,000–2,600 each. A night-only campaign spent ₹35,600 for 13 sales.

Why it burns money. Few people buy after midnight. Extra night money buys accidental clicks, not buyers.

The fix. Never run a full-budget night campaign. Keep night spend near ₹8,000–10,000 a month. Check it monthly in Campaigns › Insights and reports › When and where ads showed, by hour.

How sure. The numbers are proven. The reason is likely.

How common. GrowthSpree, a vendor, found 67.8% of overnight spend wasted across 43 B2B SaaS accounts (GrowthSpree).

6. Showing ads for searches that will never buy

What happened. In 2026, up to 17% of the searches the ads showed for had nothing to do with what the store sells.

Why it burns money. An unrelated click costs as much as a buyer's click. It never becomes an order.

The fix. Campaigns › Keywords › Negative keywords › +. Pick the Performance Max campaign and add the terms, one per line. They apply to Search and Shopping only. Review search terms every month.

How sure. Proven: a direct count.

How common. Doctor Ads, a vendor, found the median of 84 advertisers spent 39.4% of search-term cost on terms with no attributed conversion (Doctor Ads).

7. Running videos that sell nothing

What happened. 13 of 16 videos made no sale. An event video cost ₹17,400 and brought ₹95. An offer video cost ₹10,000 and brought ₹0.

Why it burns money. A video with no product gives a buyer nothing to buy.

The fix. Turn off the two weakest videos in all asset groups for 3–4 weeks (Campaigns › Asset groups › Edit › Videos). Change nothing else. If total sales drop sharply, restore them; if not, swap in short bestseller videos. Upload your own, or Google may make videos for you. Asset costs overlap to 2.6 times real spend, so use them to find duds, not to count savings.

How sure. The cost is proven. It is possible the videos help people find the brand, so test first.

How common. In AppsFlyer's vendor study of 1.1 million video ads, the top 2% of creatives took 43% of spend in non-gaming apps (AppsFlyer).

8. Leaving dud images in place

What happened. 64 of 79 images made no sale in 3 months. Google made 101 images by itself. They got 3,533 views in 8 months. The store's own images got 1,080,000.

Why it burns money. Every impression on a dud is money not spent on an image that sells.

The fix. Every 4–6 weeks, replace up to 5 no-sale images (Campaigns › Asset groups › Edit › Images). Keep every image that sold. Fill each asset group with 20 of your own. Never start a new campaign just to swap images.

How sure. Proven: direct counts.

How common. Optmyzr's vendor study of 24,702 Performance Max campaigns found 57% of advertisers used full creative sets yet saw only average results (Optmyzr).

9. Advertising products that never sell

What happened. 792 of 1,027 products took ₹51,100 in 3 months (10 Jul – 9 Oct 2026): 41% of product-ad money. They sold nothing through ads: ₹16,900 a month. The top 10% of products brought 94% of ad sales.

Why it burns money. Every asset group showed every product. Nothing told Google which ones sell.

The fix. Campaigns › Asset groups › Listing groups; click the green circle by a product, then Exclude. Start with the biggest burners. Monthly, exclude products with 3 months of clicks and no sale; spare new ones for 30 days. Keep them in the store.

How sure. Proven for ad sales. But 381 of the 792 still sold through other channels, so check store and offline sales first.

How common. SKU Analyzer, a tool vendor, says audits routinely find 20–40% of Shopping spend on zero-conversion products. It shows no sample (SKU Analyzer).

10. Letting a ₹99 add-on eat the budget

What happened. One ₹99 add-on got ₹33,644 of ads and 324,155 clicks in a year. Its ads sold nothing. The store sold 5 all year: about ₹6,700 of ads for each one. Google counted 356,081 page views from those clicks as "conversions".

Why it burns money. Cheap, colourful items draw accidental clicks in apps and videos. With page views as a goal, each one looks like a win.

The fix. Exclude its item ID from every asset group (same path as mistake 9). Never advertise cheap add-ons on their own.

How sure. The spend, clicks and zero ad sales are proven. The accidental-click reason is likely.

How common. In vendor data from Fraud Blocker (104 million clicks, to Feb 2026), 35.5% of Display and Video clicks were invalid. Shopping: 7.9%.

11. Starving your bestsellers

What happened. The top product line makes 22% of store revenue but gets 6% of ad money, while it returns ₹11.90 per ₹1. Five of the top 15 products got under ₹500 of ads all year. 11 of 40 bestsellers can't be bought today.

Why it burns money. The feed has no labels. Google can't tell a bestseller from a ₹99 add-on.

The fix. Set custom label 0 = "bestseller" on your top 50 products. Add one asset group for them in a running campaign (Campaigns › Asset groups › +), targeting that label. Restock "inactive" bestsellers. Push only products with 2+ weeks of stock.

How sure. Shares and stock counts are proven. ₹11.90 is a 3-month average; the next rupee will likely earn less.

How common. We found no public study that counts this, so we give no number. Google's own help tells advertisers to group products with custom labels (Google Ads Help).

12. Ignoring feed errors

What happened. On 10 Oct 2026, 74 of 761 products could not show. 43 were out of stock. 34 had a price that differed from the store. 8 had image problems. 2 pages were dead. Some had more than one issue.

Why it burns money. Wrong prices and dead pages hide products. Ads for products nobody can buy pay for clicks that can't convert.

The fix. Merchant Center › Products › Needs attention. Fix the 34 price mismatches first. Update the feed the same day any store price changes. Ads on unbuyable products already fell from ₹25,700 (3 months) to ₹706 (last 14 days). Keep it there.

How sure. Proven: counts from the account.

How common. DataFeedWatch, a vendor, found 7% of Shopping feed items rejected for data errors across 15,000+ stores (2022 data, via Webtonic).

How do I check my account for these mistakes in 30 minutes?

Run this 12-point mistake scan. Write down the number you find at each step.

  1. Goals (3 min). Goals › Conversions › Summary. Anything but purchases set to Primary? Do "conversions" match orders?
  2. Targets (2 min). Settings › Bidding. Any target raised over 20% in one step?
  3. Channels (3 min). Channel performance. Search at 30% or more? What did YouTube sell?
  4. Lineup (2 min). Change history. Any campaign paused while still selling?
  5. Night (2 min). Hour view. Does a night sale cost over twice a day sale?
  6. Search terms (4 min). Last 30 days. Mark terms you would never sell to.
  7. Videos (2 min). Any video with spend, no sale and no product?
  8. Images (2 min). Count images with no sale in 3 months.
  9. Products (4 min). Product report, 3 months. Sum the spend on products with zero ad sales.
  10. Cheap items (1 min). Sort by clicks. A low-price item with thousands of clicks and no sale?
  11. Bestsellers (3 min). Top 20 by revenue against their ad spend. Any out of stock or unlabelled?
  12. Feed (2 min). Merchant Center › Products › Needs attention. Count price mismatches.

Questions people ask

What is the most common Google Ads mistake?

Counting the wrong thing as a conversion. In our audit, page views were a primary goal: 816,270 conversions against 303 orders. A vendor found significant tracking issues in 70–80% of 300+ B2B SaaS accounts it audited.

How much of a Google Ads budget is usually wasted?

Often a third or more. Doctor Ads found a median 39.4% of search-term cost went to terms with no attributed conversion. GrowthSpree found 36.1% average waste in 43 B2B SaaS accounts. Our audit found 48%.

Should I stop advertising products that never sell?

Yes, after you check other sales. In our audit, 381 of 792 products with no ad sales still sold through other channels. Exclude them from ads, not the store. Give new products 30 days.

How long does it take to fix Google Ads mistakes?

Minutes to make, 2–3 weeks to settle. Make one change, then wait. Google says bidding can take up to around 50 conversion events or 3 conversion cycles to calibrate.
