Between 7 August and 9 October 2026, one store's Google Ads account reported 816,270 conversions. The store had 303 orders. Google's AI was not broken. It was chasing page views that had been set up to count as purchases.

That is the risk with every AI marketing agent: it works hard on whatever number you feed it. Here are 10 real agents, their prices as of October 2026, and the job none of them does yet.

Agent vs automation vs copilot — what's the difference?

Automation follows rules you wrote. A copilot suggests and waits for you. An agent takes a goal, decides the steps, changes live things and checks the result.

  • Automation: "If cost per sale passes my limit, pause the ad." Fast and predictable. It never asks why.
  • Copilot (assistant): you ask, it drafts, you click apply. Optmyzr (from $209 a month, per its pricing page) honestly calls its AI Sidekick a copilot.
  • Agent: you set the goal and limits. It plans, acts on a schedule and reports back. You approve the big moves.

The label is cheap. Gartner estimates "only about 130 of the thousands of agentic AI vendors are real," and expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by the end of 2027 (June 2025 forecast, reported by Outlook Business). Gartner calls this relabelling of chatbots and assistants agent washing.

One test: ask what the tool changes without you clicking. If the answer is nothing, it is a copilot. Pay copilot prices.

Which AI agents run your paid ads?

Five ad agents made the list: two come free inside the ad platforms, three sit on top. For a Google-only shortlist, read Best AI tools for Google Ads in 2026.

1. Google AI Max for Search + Ask Advisor

  • What it does: AI Max matches more search terms, rewrites ad text and picks the landing page. Ask Advisor, Google's cross-product agent (announced 20 May 2026, beta for English-language accounts), can pull products from Merchant Center and set up a campaign from a plain request.
  • Price (as of October 2026): no fee. You pay for the ads.
  • Best for: Search-led advertisers on broad match or Performance Max (Google's campaign type that buys Search, YouTube and Display from one budget).
  • Honest limit: it optimises the conversions Google counts. Google claims about 14% more conversions at a similar cost (its own 2025 data). With a wrong goal, that is 14% more of the wrong thing.

2. Meta Advantage+ and the Meta AI business assistant

  • What it does: Advantage+ sets audience, placement and budget for sales, app and leads campaigns. In October 2026, Meta said its Ads Manager assistant is getting agent actions: creative, targeting and budget changes from a chat prompt (Marketing Dive, 8 Oct 2026). Outside agents can act through its Ads MCP server.
  • Price (as of October 2026): no fee. You pay for the ads.
  • Best for: D2C and app brands with clean purchase tracking.
  • Honest limit: Meta grades its own homework: its claim of 20% better cost per action on sales campaigns is its own figure. And it will never move your money to Google.

3. Smartly

  • What it does: an enterprise platform whose agents plan, launch and optimise ads across social, 200+ streaming TV services and open-web buying (Smartly). In June 2026 it added Synapse, a shared memory that feeds those agents lessons from past campaigns (PPC Land, 22 Jun 2026).
  • Price (as of October 2026): on request (demo only).
  • Best for: large brands and agencies. Smartly says it manages over $7 billion of ad spend for 800+ brands.
  • Honest limit: built for big teams and big budgets. No public price.

4. Madgicx

  • What it does: its AI Marketer audits a Meta account and lists the next steps. Other modules launch creatives and scale winners automatically (Madgicx). It acts on Meta only; reports also pull Google Ads, GA4, Shopify, Klaviyo and TikTok.
  • Price (as of October 2026): the main plan price shows only inside the app; 7-day free trial; tracking add-on $49 a month per account (pricing page).
  • Best for: Shopify and D2C brands where Meta is the main channel.
  • Honest limit: one platform for actions, and its pages describe no approval step.

5. Ryze

  • What it does: agents audit accounts around the clock and fix bids, budgets, creative and wasted spend on Google, Meta, Microsoft, TikTok, LinkedIn and more, plus SEO and landing pages (Ryze).
  • Price (as of October 2026): Paid Ads $89 a month, Agency $279 a month, 7-day trial (pricing page).
  • Best for: agencies and lean teams with many accounts.
  • Honest limit: approval on every change is listed in all plans, but its site describes no undo once a change is live.

Which AI agents run email, CRM and content?

HubSpot and Salesforce run agents inside the CRM, where your contacts and deals live. Jasper runs agents for content.

6. HubSpot Breeze agents (now Agent Hub)

  • What it does: HubSpot's agents now sit in Agent Hub. Content Agent writes blogs, social posts and landing pages; Campaign Agent turns a goal into a multi-channel plan; Nurture and Prospecting agents email leads and start outreach. Several are in beta.
  • Price (as of October 2026): Marketing Hub Starter $20 per seat a month with 500 HubSpot Credits; Professional from $890 a month (3 seats, 3,000 credits). Agents spend credits; extra credits cost $10 per 1,000 (HubSpot catalog).
  • Best for: small and mid-size teams already on HubSpot.
  • Honest limit: it sees only what is in HubSpot and does not run your Google or Meta budgets. Credit costs grow with use.

7. Salesforce Agentforce Marketing

  • What it does: in Marketing Cloud Next, the Campaign Agent takes your goals and guardrails, creates content, decides what to send and adjusts sends and budgets as the campaign runs, across email, SMS and web (Salesforce).
  • Price (as of October 2026): Growth $1,500 per org a month, Advanced $3,250, billed annually. Paid-media optimisation is an add-on at $10,000 per org a month (pricing page).
  • Best for: enterprises whose customer data already sits in Salesforce.
  • Honest limit: enterprise price, enterprise setup. Small teams pay for far more than they use.

8. Jasper

  • What it does: a content workspace with 100+ specialised agents for SEO and AI-search content, campaigns, email, social, personalisation and research (Jasper).
  • Price (as of October 2026): Pro $69 per seat a month, or $59 billed yearly; Business is custom (pricing page).
  • Best for: content teams that need on-brand volume. More options in Best AI agents for content marketing.
  • Honest limit: the custom-agent builder and agents for complex work sit on the custom-priced Business plan. It makes content; it does not move ad money.

Which AI agents run outbound?

Clay and Copy.ai build lists, research accounts and write outreach. For agents that also book meetings, read Best AI SDR agents in 2026.

9. Clay

  • What it does: a data and workflow canvas for go-to-market (GTM) teams, the people who find and win customers. Claygent, its research agent, browses the web for any data point. Plays push top accounts into email sequences and LinkedIn, Meta and Google ad audiences (Clay).
  • Price (as of October 2026): free plan (500 actions a month); Launch $167 and Growth $446 a month on the plan cards; costs rise with credits (pricing page).
  • Best for: B2B teams with a technical operator.
  • Honest limit: someone must build and maintain the workflows. No builder, no value.

10. Copy.ai (now part of Fullcast)

  • What it does: workflows and agents for prospecting, content, lead handling and CRM enrichment, with brand-voice guardrails (Copy.ai). Fullcast bought it in October 2025 and sells it as Fullcast Propel (Fullcast).
  • Price (as of October 2026): Chat $29 a month; workflow credits start with Growth at $1,000 a month, billed yearly (pricing page).
  • Best for: sales and marketing ops teams that want repeatable workflows.
  • Honest limit: a big jump from $29 to $1,000, and the roadmap now follows a revenue-operations suite.

What can no off-the-shelf AI marketing agent do?

None of the 10 decides across platforms on your real sales. Each optimises the number its own platform or CRM counts. One real audit of an Indian D2C (direct-to-consumer) store shows the cost:

  • Google counted 816,270 conversions from 7 August to 9 October 2026. The store had 303 orders: about 2,694 "conversions" per real order.
  • Page views were a goal, so Google counted them as purchases: 64–89% of the value it bid for, every month since July 2025. It is one of the 12 common Google Ads mistakes.
  • One Performance Max campaign showed 32,485 conversions in a week. It made 5 sales.
  • Ad spend rose 96% in a year. Online revenue rose 31%.
  • 48% of the ₹71,800 monthly budget (₹34,400) sold nothing.
  • Over 3 months, product ads on Search returned ₹10.60 per ₹1; on YouTube, ₹0.01. YouTube still got 56% of that money.

Google's AI did its job. The goal was wrong. A tool that trusts the platform's count sees a winner.

To catch it, an agent must do three things we did not find on any of the 10 tools' own pages:

  1. Match ad results to real orders by day and value. Here, only 234 of 450 store orders in 3 months matched a Google purchase (see the proof).
  2. Compare cost per real order across Google, Meta, email and outbound, then move money between them.
  3. Check waste at three levels: campaign, asset and product. Here, 792 of 1,027 products took ad money in 3 months and never sold through ads.

How do you choose an AI marketing agent? Use this 12-question scorecard

Score each tool 0, 1 or 2 on these 12 questions; shortlist only tools that reach 18 of 24. Copy the table and ask every vendor the same questions.

1What does it change without me clicking?Names each action; you switch each on or off.
2Can every change wait for my approval?Approval by type of change, with your limits.
3Can I undo a change from the tool?One-click undo, logged.
4Which number does it optimise?Real orders, revenue or pipeline, not platform conversions.

Read your score. 18–24: pilot one job with a spend cap. 12–17: use it as a copilot, at copilot prices. Under 12: walk away. On their own pages, most tools here lose points on questions 3, 4 and 5.

Questions people ask

What is the best AI agent for marketing in 2026?

It depends on the job. Google AI Max and Meta Advantage+ cost nothing beyond ad spend. Ryze starts at $89 a month across ad platforms. HubSpot and Salesforce lead for email and CRM, Jasper for content, and Clay and Copy.ai for outbound.

What is the difference between an AI agent and marketing automation?

Automation follows rules you wrote, such as pausing an ad when cost per sale passes a limit. An AI agent takes a goal, decides the steps, changes live campaigns or emails, and checks the result. A copilot only suggests and waits for your click.

How much do AI marketing agents cost in 2026?

As of October 2026, Google and Meta charge no fee beyond ad spend. Self-serve plans here run from $20 per seat a month (HubSpot Starter) to $1,000 a month (Copy.ai Growth). Salesforce starts at $1,500 per org a month. Credits and seats add to the bill.

Can AI agents replace a marketing team?

They replace busywork, not judgment. Agents run checks, reports, drafts and routine budget moves every day. People set the goal and approve big changes. In one audit, Google's AI chased a wrong goal for months: 816,270 conversions against 303 orders.

Do AI marketing agents ask for approval before they make changes?

Some do. Ryze lists approval on every change. Meta's Ads MCP server, the official way for outside AI tools to work in Meta ad accounts, can start read-only or approval-required. Few vendor pages describe undo, so ask for approval and undo in writing.
